Use the contents on the left to jump straight to what you need. The transliteration table further down is loaded live from the database, so it always reflects the project's current spelling conventions.

# Welcome to Naadaalay DB

Naadaalay DB is an attempt to treat Hindustaanee raagdaaree sangeet -- specifically the raag, its melodic framework -- as something that can be catalogued with the same rigor as any other structured body of knowledge, without losing sight of the fact that a raag is, first and foremost, a musical and emotional experience, not just a scale.

Every raag in this database is reachable from first principles: a combined svarset (which pitches are used at all), an aaroh and avaroh (the paths taken up and down), and from those two simple facts, a whole cascade of derived structural properties -- jaati, thaat, symmetry, samvaad, moorchhanaa family, and dozens more -- calculated the same way, every time, for every raag, whether it's Yaman or something far more obscure. That consistency is the whole point: it means two raags can be honestly compared, searched, and filtered against each other on their actual musical structure, not just on how well-documented they happen to be.

None of this replaces the tradition itself, a teacher, or years of riyaaz. It's meant as a companion -- a way to ask precise questions ("which raags share Bhairav's svarset but invert its samvaad?") that would otherwise mean flipping through a dozen books, and to make space for raags that are structurally valid and interesting even if they've never been formally named or performed.

how it's built

Every calculated column -- thaat, jaati, symmetry, samvaad, and so on -- is generated once by a program that works through every valid aaroh/avaroh combination, and is never edited by hand afterward. Only the human layer of a raag's entry -- its name, creator, samay, and notes -- is entered manually, and can change as research or performance practice reveals more.

# Transcription, Not Transliteration

Every romanized spelling on this site -- raag names, thaat names, terms like svarsthaan or samvaad -- is built to transcribe how the word is actually said in everyday Hindustaanee musical speech, not to transliterate its classical Devanaagaree spelling letter by letter. Those two goals usually agree, but not always -- and wherever they disagree, this project always chooses the sound over the spelling.

The clearest place this shows up is a phenomenon linguists call schwa deletion. Every Devanaagaree consonant carries an inherent, unwritten "a" sound by default -- क on its own is read "ka", not just "k". But in spoken Hindi, that inherent "a" is very often silent, especially at the end of a word, even though nothing in the written Devanaagaree marks this difference. थाट, read strictly letter-by-letter, would be "thaata" -- but nobody says that; it's said "thaat". So Naadaalay DB spells it thaaT, not thaaTa.

The same thing happens constantly in raag names you likely already know:

DevanaagareeRead letter-by-letterActually saidNaadaalay DB spelling
थाटthaatathaatthaaT
भैरवbhairavabhairavBhairav
यमनyamanayamanYaman

This project's spellings always follow the third column, not the second.

common misconception

Don't expect every Devanaagaree akshara to correspond to one roman syllable in a displayed name. A name here is a transcription of pronunciation, not a mechanical letter-count of the original spelling -- some written vowels are silent, and this scheme doesn't write them.

# Reading the Spellings

A quick recap of the rules that produce every spelling on the site:

  • Long vowels are doubled: aa, ee, oo. Short vowels are single: a, i, u.
  • Aspirated consonants get a trailing h: kh, gh, jh, bh, and so on.
  • Retroflex consonants are CAPITALIZED: T Th D Dh N L. Nothing else is capitalized, with two explicit exceptions: Sh for ष, and R for ऋ.
  • Conjoint (compound) letters mostly just spell out their parts: त्र = tr, श्र = shr. Two are irregular and kept as fixed traditional spellings: क्ष = ksh, ज्ञ = jñ.
two more exceptions

Two marks needed capital letters that fall outside the retroflex/ष/ऋ list above, purely to avoid colliding with spellings that already existed: M for anusvaar (ं) and H for visarga (ः). Neither ever appears standalone anywhere else in this scheme, so there's no ambiguity.

# Confidence Ratings

Every raag row carries a confidence rating from 1 to 10, reflecting how solidly its non-computed information -- its name, attribution, samay, and notes -- is actually established, as opposed to being a reasonable but less certain guess.

This matters because of how the database is built: every structurally valid combination of aaroh and avaroh gets a row, whether or not it corresponds to a raag that's actually named, attested, or performed anywhere in the tradition. The computed columns (thaat, jaati, symmetry, and so on) are always exactly correct by construction -- they're arithmetic. The confidence rating is about everything else: how sure we can be that this specific svarset really is the raag it's labeled as, rather than a plausible but coincidental match.

Roughly: a 1–3 marks a tentative or single-source identification; 4–7 marks a reasonably well-attested raag with some remaining uncertainty (a disputed creator, a name that varies by gharaanaa); 8–10 marks a raag whose identity, name, and classification are essentially beyond dispute -- the Yamans and Bhairavs of the tradition.
