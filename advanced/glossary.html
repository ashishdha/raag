Hindustaanee Music Glossary

Every Hindustaanee music term used across Naadaalay DB — the Raag Explorer, its User Guide, and the database itself — defined here in one place. Entries are grouped by theme rather than strict alphabetical order, since most terms make more sense next to their close relatives. Arriving from a link elsewhere on the site will jump straight to, and briefly highlight, the relevant entry. Within each definition, closely related terms are cross-linked to each other.

1 Core concepts

Raag noun

A melodic framework in Hindustaanee (North Indian) classical music — more than just a scale, a raag defines which svar may be used, how they typically move in ascent (aaroh) and descent (avaroh), which notes get special emphasis, and the mood it's meant to evoke. Naadaalay DB catalogs both established raags and computationally-generated candidates that satisfy the same structural rules but haven't necessarily been performed or named.

Svar noun

A note. Hindustaanee music divides the octave into twelve svarsthaan, grouped into seven svar "families" — Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, and Ni — the same seven syllables used throughout the sargam solfège system.

Svarsthaan noun

A specific pitch position within the octave — literally "note-place." While there are only seven svar families, five of them (Re, Ga, Ma, Dha, Ni) have two possible svarsthaan each — a shuddha form and an altered form — giving twelve svarsthaan in total per octave.

Svarset noun

The complete collection of svarsthaan used by a raag, combining whatever appears in its aaroh and its avaroh into one unordered set. Two raags can share an identical svarset while still sounding completely different, purely because of how each one actually moves through those notes.

2 The seven svar names

Sa Shadja

The tonic — the fixed reference pitch every other svar is measured against. Sa has no komal or teevra variant; it's always the same relative pitch, tuned to whatever the performer's instrument (or taanpuraa drone) is set to.

Re Rishabh

The second svar. Has two forms: komal re (a semitone above Sa) and shuddha Re (a whole tone above Sa).

Ga Gandhaar

The third svar. Has two forms: komal ga and shuddha Ga.

Ma Madhyam

The fourth svar, and the midpoint of the octave. Unlike Re, Ga, Dha and Ni, Ma has no komal form — instead its two variants are shuddha M (a perfect fourth above Sa) and teevra m (an augmented fourth, a semitone higher). See teevra.

Pa Pancham

The fifth svar. Like Sa, Pa has no altered form in the classical system — it's always a perfect fifth above Sa.

Dha Dhaivat

The sixth svar. Has two forms: komal d and shuddha D.

Ni Nishaad

The seventh svar. Has two forms: komal n and shuddha N.

3 Svar variants & notation

Shuddha adjective

Natural, or "pure." In sargam notation, shuddha svar are written uppercase. For every svar except Ma, shuddha is the higher of its two possible positions; for Ma specifically, shuddha is the lower of the two — see teevra.

Komal adjective

Flat — lowered by a semitone from the shuddha position. Written lowercase in sargam notation. Applies to Re, Ga, Dha and Ni; Sa, Ma and Pa have no komal form.

Teevra adjective

Sharp — specifically, the raised form of Ma (written lowercase m), a semitone above shuddha Ma. Ma is the only svar with a teevra variant instead of a komal one.

Sargam noun

The solfège naming system used throughout Hindustaanee music: S r R g G M m P d D n N, one letter per svarsthaan, with case distinguishing shuddha from komal/teevra. The word comes from its first four syllables — Sa, Re, Ga, Ma.

4 Melodic movement

Aaroh noun

The ascending movement of a raag — the specific path its notes take on the way up, which isn't always simply "every used note in order." Some raags skip, on the way up, notes they use on the way down (or vice versa). Compare avaroh.

Avaroh noun

The descending movement of a raag — the mirror of aaroh. Comparing a raag's aaroh and avaroh — whether they use the same notes, in the same shape — is one of the main ways Naadaalay DB classifies raags; see dimension.

Varjit svar noun

Literally "forbidden notes" — a svar family deliberately left out of a raag entirely, appearing in neither the aaroh nor the avaroh. Leaving out one or more svar is part of what gives many raags their distinct character.

Vakra adjective

Crooked, or zigzagging — melodic movement that doesn't proceed in strict ascending or descending order, dipping back to an earlier note before continuing onward. Naadaalay DB uses Vakra as one of its five dimension categories.

5 Scale framework & classification

Thaat noun

A parent scale framework used to group raags by their underlying note-choices, independent of a raag's specific aaroh/avaroh shape. Classical pedagogy (the Bhaatkhande system) recognizes ten named thaat — Bilaaval, Kalyaan, Khamaaj, Bhairav, Poorvee, Maarwa, Kaafee, Aasaavaree, Bhairavee, and Todee — but a thaat is really just one combination of which of the five variable svar (Re, Ga, Ma, Dha, Ni) take their shuddha or altered form, so there are 32 such combinations overall. Naadaalay DB tracks all 32, not only the ten with traditional names.

Moorchhanaa noun

A modal rotation — shifting which svarsthaan is treated as Sa within an existing collection of notes, producing a related but distinct-sounding scale (the Hindustaanee equivalent of what Western theory calls a "mode"). Naadaalay DB groups every scale into a moorchhanaa family, headed by whichever rotation is considered the most fundamental — the most shuddha — form.

Jaati noun

How many of the seven svar families a raag's aaroh or avaroh actually uses. See audav, shadav, and sampoorna for the classical names of the common counts.

Audav noun

The classical term for a jaati of five — a pentatonic raag, using only five of the seven svar families in a given direction.

Shadav noun

The classical term for a jaati of six — a hexatonic raag, using six of the seven svar families.

Sampoorna adjective

"Complete" — the classical term for a jaati of seven, meaning all seven svar families are used in that direction.

6 Relationships & register

Samvaad noun

A consonance relationship between two svar a fourth or fifth apart — in Hindustaanee theory these pairings (most importantly Sa and Pa, and Sa and Ma) are considered especially harmonious, and traditionally one such pair is chosen as a raag's most emphasized notes. Naadaalay DB scores how strongly a raag's note-set preserves the Sa-Pa and Sa-Ma relationships specifically.

Ang noun

Literally "limb" or "section." Used here two ways: to describe a raag's overall melodic personality or characteristic mood-cluster — a classification that goes beyond thaat or svarset alone — and, more literally, to mean one half of the octave; see poorvaang and uttaraang.

Poorvaang noun

The lower half of the octave — Sa through Ma. ("Poorva" means "earlier" or "former.") Compare uttaraang.

Uttaraang noun

The upper half of the octave — Pa through the octave's Sa. ("Uttara" means "later" or "latter.") Compare poorvaang.

Samay noun

The traditional time of day (or, for a few raags, season) a raag is meant to be performed in — part of a broader belief that certain melodic moods suit certain hours, tied to the eight three-hour prahar of the traditional day, or to the six shad-ritu seasons.

7 Raag relationship types dimension

Dimension noun

A Naadaalay DB classification describing how a raag's aaroh relates to its avaroh: Identical (the same notes, same shape, both ways), Different (a different note-set each way), vakra (zigzagging movement), joD (built by joining two fragments), or raagish (a borderline, not-fully-confirmed case).

JoD noun

Literally "pair" or "join" — a dimension category for raags built by combining two distinct melodic fragments.

Raagish adjective

Raag-like, but not fully confirmed as a proper raag — a borderline dimension classification for candidates that show clear raag-like structure without, yet, meeting every traditional criterion.

8 Traditions

Hindustaanee adjective / noun

The North Indian classical music tradition, as distinct from Carnatic, the classical tradition of South India. Naadaalay DB's raags, terminology, and notation are all drawn from the Hindustaanee system.

Carnatic adjective / noun

The classical music tradition of South India. Its solfège system uses syllables similar to Hindustaanee sargam but different rules for which notes can combine, and different names for many of its scales. The Raag Explorer's Carnatic Sargam notation option shows the closest equivalent spelling in that system, for comparison — it does not mean the raag itself is a Carnatic composition.

A note on spelling

Naadaalay DB uses precise, doubled-vowel phonetic transliteration throughout — aa, ee, and oo represent long vowel sounds, so "raag" (not "raga"), "sargam," "Kaafee," and "Bhairavee" are spelled deliberately, not inconsistently.
